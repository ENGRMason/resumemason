Automated GPS Heads-Up Display (H.U.D.)

TypePersonal ProjectDateJune 2023

A wearable heads-up display for downhill sports, showing live speed, altitude, and elapsed time in the rider's field of view so the data is glanceable mid-run instead of something you check at the bottom of the hill.

Key figures
ComputeRaspberry Pi Zero W, Raspberry Pi OS Lite
DisplayLCD projected onto polarized lenses
DataLive speed, altitude, elapsed time from GPS
FrameRigid PLA chassis, flexible TPU contact surfaces
CADFusion 360

System

A Raspberry Pi Zero W drives an LCD that projects onto polarized lenses. The Pi was chosen for size and weight, since anything mounted to goggles is carried on your face for hours. A GPS module supplies position and velocity to a Python interface controlling the display, running Raspberry Pi OS Lite to keep overhead minimal.

Two materials, one frame

The frame went through extensive iteration in Fusion 360 using a hybrid-material approach: rigid PLA for the structural chassis carrying the Pi, display, and battery, and flexible TPU for the sections that conform to the face and helmet. No single material solved both requirements. TPU took significant troubleshooting with reduced print speeds and cooling disabled.

Weight distribution

Comfort turned out to be the binding constraint. A front-heavy assembly becomes uncomfortable fast and is impractical for all-day use, which makes battery and compute placement a balance problem before it is a packaging problem.

Tools and methods

Python, Raspberry Pi, Fusion 360, FDM printing in PLA and TPU, GPS
