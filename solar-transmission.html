Custom Solar Plant Transmission

TypeUniversity Project (Group)DateFall 2024

A team-designed high-power speed reduction gearbox for a concentrated solar power plant in Nevada, produced as a design study for the Crescent Dunes project owners. The transmission couples a 6,000 RPM steam turbine to a 450 RPM generator across a 13.3:1 reduction, serving a 30 MW plant split into two parallel 15 MW turbine lines.

Duty cycle, not peak power

The governing design problem was duty cycle rather than peak power. The CSP plant operates nightly from 5 PM to 5 AM on stored thermal energy, delivering fluctuating torque instead of a steady load. Over a 25-year service life, that fluctuation creates a fatigue problem long before strength becomes limiting.

We worked from plant-level requirements down to shaft-by-shaft targets: roughly 16.276 MW at 6,000 RPM on the input shaft against 331.6 kNm at 450 RPM on the output, sizing a multi-stage geartrain at an assumed 96 percent overall efficiency to account for realistic drivetrain losses.

Designing for the maintenance window

The complete mechanical package included shafts, gears, bearings, couplings, and a serviceable two-piece housing with lubrication fill and drain provisions. The design targets infinite life with a minimum 1.4 safety factor.

Because such plants generate no revenue during downtime, we documented assembly sequencing and a planned bearing replacement and overhaul schedule, treating maintainability as a core design requirement rather than an afterthought.
