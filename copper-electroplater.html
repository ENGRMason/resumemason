Copper Electroplater Development

TypePersonal BuildDateOngoing

A copper electroplating setup that deposits solid metal onto 3D printed plastic parts, turning PLA prints into pieces with genuine metal surfaces rather than metallic paint.

Key figures
SubstratePLA 3D prints
PrepPrimer smoothing, then graphite conductive layer
AnodeSacrificial thin copper sheet
ElectrolyteCopper solution
RateDeliberately slow to avoid edge roughness

Making an insulator conductive

The interesting problem is that electroplating requires a conductive substrate and plastic is an insulator, so the process starts before any current flows. Prints get a primer coat for surface smoothing, then graphite application to create conductivity.

That prep stage determines almost everything about the result. An uneven or poorly adhered conductive layer produces patchy plating no amount of time in the tank will correct.

The bath

The bath runs off a copper electrolyte solution with thin copper sheet as the sacrificial anode, dissolving into solution as it plates out onto the part. Deposition is deliberately slow. Driven hard, plating builds unevenly and roughly at edges and high-current areas.

Safety

Dedicated ventilation and proper chemical disposal, both built in from the start. Working with chemistry outside an institutional setting means the safety and waste side is your responsibility alone.

Where it came from

It started with an interest in steampunk aesthetics and wanting components that were authentically metallic rather than painted. The goal now is scaling capacity toward a fully copper-plated robot.
